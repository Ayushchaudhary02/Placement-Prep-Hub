Pillar 02

3 Projects That Will Get You Hired

Certifications tell recruiters what you studied. Projects prove what you can do. There is a difference.

❌ What most students do

  • 10 certifications with no projects
  • Tutorial clones with no original thought
  • Projects that don't work when demoed

✅ What gets you hired

  • 3 working projects on GitHub
  • At least one solves a real problem
  • A clear README with screenshots
01
💡

A Real-Use Project

Solves a real problem

Something that fixes a small daily frustration. A hostel complaint tracker. A college timetable manager. A local shop inventory app. Even basic — it shows initiative.

Hostel Complaint App
Timetable Manager
Shop Inventory
College Event Tracker
💬 In the interview say: "I built this because I personally faced this problem at college." That one sentence is worth more than 3 certifications.
Difficulty: Beginner Friendly
Time: 2-3 weeks
02
🔧

A Domain-Relevant Project

Proves your track skills

Directly tied to your chosen track. ML track: sentiment analyzer or price predictor. Web track: full CRUD app with login system. Simple, working, and well-documented.

Sentiment Analyzer (ML)
CRUD App with Auth (Web)
Auto-deploy Pipeline (DevOps)
Weather App (Mobile)
💬 This project answers: "Have you used [technology] before?" without you saying a word.
Difficulty: Intermediate
Time: 3-4 weeks
03
🌍

An Open-Source Contribution

Proves you can work in a team

Even fixing a typo in documentation counts. It shows you can read other people's code and collaborate — something interviewers value enormously.

1. Find a beginner-friendly repo
→
2. Read CONTRIBUTING.md
→
3. Fix a bug or improve docs
→
4. Submit a pull request
💬 Say in any interview: "I contributed to an open-source project." Watch the interviewer's reaction.
Difficulty: Any level
Time: 1-2 days

Pillar 03

English Is a Skill, Not a Talent

You don't need to speak like a news anchor. You need to explain your code clearly. That is it.

My English is too bad. I can never improve it. I will always lose to students from English medium schools.
IT companies in India need functional English — emails, stand-ups, reading docs. Anyone can learn this in 90 days with daily practice.
01

Reading — Start Today, Free

30 min/day

Read one short English article daily. Google News India (English) or GeeksforGeeks articles. Don't look up every word — guess meaning from context. 30 days minimum.

Google News India
GeeksforGeeks
TechCrunch India
Day 1 of 30
02

Speaking Practice — The Hardest, Most Important

2 min/day

Record yourself explaining a DSA concept in English for 2 minutes daily. Don't judge the recording. Just do it. After 30 days, compare first and last recording.

• Explain what a linked list is
• Describe how BFS works
• Explain recursion to a 10-year-old
Your Day 1 recording will be terrible. That is the point. Your Day 30 recording will surprise you.
03

Writing — Most Practical Workplace Skill

15 min/day

Practice writing professional emails and short technical explanations. Use Claude to correct your writing and explain mistakes. Focus on clarity over grammar.

Subject: Query regarding [topic] Hi [Name], I wanted to ask about [specific thing]. Could you please help me with [request]? Thank you, [Your name]
04

Watch Technical English — Learn Two Things at Once

20 min/day

YouTube channels where you absorb technical English AND coding knowledge simultaneously. Always watch with English subtitles.

🎬
Traversy Media

05

Think in English — 10 Min/Day

10 min/day

While walking, eating, or commuting — narrate what you're doing in English in your head. This builds the mental pathway that makes speaking feel natural.

"I am opening my laptop. I am going to study graphs today. Let me start by understanding what a graph is. A graph is a data structure that..."

The 30-Day English Challenge

5 minutes of reading + 2 minutes of speaking recording + 1 email draft. Every day for 30 days. By day 30 you will not recognize your own improvement.

Pillar 04

Recruiters Are Searching For You Right Now

The question is whether they can find you. Visibility is not about showing off — it is about being findable.

🔗

LinkedIn Profile

Most Important for Service Companies
  • Add a professional photo (not a selfie)
  • Write a headline: "CS Student | Full Stack Developer | Seeking Placement 2025"
  • Fill Education, Skills, and Projects sections completely
  • Add project screenshots and GitHub links
  • Connect with 50+ classmates, seniors, and professors
  • Post once a week about what you are learning
87% of recruiters check LinkedIn before calling a candidate
🐙

GitHub Activity

Most Important for Product Companies
  • Complete your profile bio with your track
  • Pin your 3 best projects
  • Write a README for every repo
  • Commit something every day — green squares matter
  • Write meaningful commit messages
  • Star and fork repos in your domain
A GitHub with 90 days of green squares tells a recruiter you are serious before you say a word
⚡

Competitive Coding

Required for Product Companies
  • Create a LeetCode account
  • Solve at least 100 problems (50 easy, 40 medium, 10 hard)
  • Aim for rating 1500+ for service companies
  • Earn at least 1 LeetCode badge
  • Try weekly contests
  • Link your profile on your resume
LeetCode rating 1500+ on a resume skips you past the initial screening in most companies
